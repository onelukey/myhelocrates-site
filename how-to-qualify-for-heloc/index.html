How to Qualify for a HELOC —
What Lenders May Review

Credit score, home equity, debt-to-income ratio, income verification — everything you need to know about HELOC eligibility before you apply.

There is no universal HELOC qualification checklist

HELOC lenders do not all use one nationwide set of approval thresholds. A lender can set its own underwriting standards, product limits and documentation requirements, so a credit score, debt-to-income ratio or home-equity percentage quoted elsewhere should not be treated as a guarantee of approval or denial.

A HELOC lets you borrow against available equity in your home, and the home secures the debt. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) warns that if you cannot keep up with required payments, you could lose your home.

Why we removed the “magic numbers”

MyHelocRates.com previously displayed fixed figures such as a 620 minimum credit score, 80–85% maximum CLTV and 43–50% maximum DTI as though they applied broadly. Those figures may appear in individual lender programs, but they are not universal HELOC rules established by the CFPB. This guide now separates general qualification factors from lender-specific underwriting standards.

Credit history and creditworthiness

A lender may use credit information when evaluating an application. Regulation B permits creditors to consider information relevant to creditworthiness, subject to rules prohibiting discrimination and restricting the use of certain information.

There is no single CFPB-established minimum credit score for every HELOC. Ask each lender whether its particular product has a minimum score and how credit profile affects pricing or eligibility.

Home equity and borrowing capacity

Home equity is the difference between the home's value and amounts already owed against it. A useful measure is combined loan-to-value (CLTV):

CLTV formula

(Existing mortgage balances + proposed HELOC amount) ÷ home value × 100 = CLTV%

For example, a $400,000 home with a $200,000 mortgage and a proposed $100,000 HELOC would have a 75% CLTV. That calculation describes the equity position; it does not make 75% an approval threshold. The lender decides the maximum CLTV allowed by its product.

A lender may use an appraisal or another valuation method. CFPB guidance identifies appraisal charges as one type of closing cost that may apply under some HELOC plans, but costs and methods vary. Ask the lender what valuation it requires and what you would pay.

Income, debts and ability to repay

Lenders can evaluate financial information when assessing creditworthiness. Regulation B also limits how certain income may be treated. For example, a creditor may not automatically discount or exclude income simply because it comes from part-time work, retirement benefits or public assistance; the applicant's individual circumstances and the likely continuation of income matter.

Rather than assuming every lender uses the same DTI ceiling or requires exactly two years of employment history, ask how the lender evaluates your existing obligations, proposed HELOC payment and the income you want considered.

How to prepare before applying

  • Review your credit reports. Check for inaccurate information and use the appropriate dispute process if something is wrong.
  • Estimate your equity. Gather your current mortgage balance and a reasonable estimate of your home's value.
  • List your existing obligations. Knowing your recurring debts helps you discuss affordability with prospective lenders.
  • Organize financial records. Ask each lender exactly what it requires rather than assuming every application uses the same document list.
  • Compare lenders. Compare rate structure, fees, draw and repayment rules as well as eligibility.

Documents a lender may request

The exact list depends on the lender and your circumstances. A lender may ask for identity information, income records, mortgage or lien information, property information, insurance or title information, and other financial records needed to verify the application. Ask the lender for its own checklist.
