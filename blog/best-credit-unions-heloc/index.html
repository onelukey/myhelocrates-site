Best Credit Unions for a HELOC? How to Compare Options

Credit unions can be worth including in a HELOC search, but rates, fees, membership rules and geographic availability vary. Here is a source-checked way to compare them.

Mike Lucas — Founder Last reviewed: September 24, 2026 9 min read
Last reviewed: September 24, 2026
There is no permanent “best” credit union for every HELOC borrower.

The useful comparison is the offer available to you: membership eligibility, property location, APR and margin, introductory pricing, fees, credit-line limits, draw and repayment rules, and the lender's current underwriting requirements.

Credit unions are member-owned financial cooperatives, and some offer HELOC products that are worth comparing with banks and other lenders. But “credit union” does not automatically mean lower rates, lower fees or easier approval. Those details are product-specific and can change.

This guide therefore does not rank lenders from best to worst or promise that one institution will be cheapest for you. Instead, we use current official lender pages as examples of the differences a homeowner should check before applying.

How we review credit union HELOCs

For lender-specific facts, we use the credit union's own current product pages, rate pages and disclosures. We do not treat an old advertised APR as a permanent product feature, and we do not infer your eligibility or likely rate from another borrower's offer.

Credit unions with HELOCs to research

The institutions below are examples with HELOC information we verified from their official websites on the review date. They are not ranked, and inclusion is not a recommendation or endorsement.

PenFed Credit Union

Example to research

PenFed currently advertises a HELOC on its official site. Its product page should be checked for the current APR and terms before applying. One especially important detail missing from the previous version of this article is the fee schedule: PenFed's current service-fee page lists an annual HELOC fee of $99.

Navy Federal Credit Union

Membership eligibility applies

Navy Federal's current HELOC page advertises a variable-rate line, a 20-year draw period followed by a 20-year repayment period, and borrowing subject to its stated equity limits and underwriting. Its published “as low as” APR is based on specified assumptions, so it should not be treated as the rate every member receives.

Navy Federal also publishes specific membership eligibility requirements. Confirm both membership and property eligibility directly before comparing its product with alternatives.

Check Navy Federal's current HELOC terms and disclosures · Check membership eligibility

Alliant Credit Union

Geographic restrictions apply

Alliant currently offers an interest-only HELOC with a 10-year draw period and subsequent repayment period. Its disclosures are a good example of why the fine print matters: HELOC availability is limited to listed states and Washington, D.C.; maximum CLTV can vary by membership tenure, property location and creditworthiness; and some states have lower limits.

The previous version of this article said Alliant had no annual fee. Its current disclosure instead says a $50 annual fee is waived for the first year and charged annually thereafter. It also describes possible early-termination and other fees.

Check Alliant's current HELOC page and disclosures

First Tech Federal Credit Union

Multiple HELOC structures

First Tech currently publishes HELOC rates and offers a Flexity line as well as other home-equity options. Its Flexity HELOC includes a variable-rate structure and the ability to lock qualifying balances into fixed-rate loans under the line. The official rate page explains that pricing depends on factors including creditworthiness, loan amount, combined loan-to-value, occupancy, property type and lien position.

First Tech's fee information also shows why “no annual fee” should never be assumed: its published materials state that the annual fee for a Flexity HELOC varies and borrowers should check their loan documents.

Why there is no rate leaderboard here

Advertised HELOC APRs can change and often depend on assumptions about credit profile, lien position, CLTV, property, loan amount, automatic payments or introductory periods. A static ranking can become misleading quickly. Use each lender's current disclosures for the actual comparison date.

How to compare two credit union HELOC offers

Start with the actual disclosures rather than the headline rate. Put the offers side by side and compare the same features.

QuestionWhat to record
What is the variable-rate formula?The index, margin, adjustment frequency, floor and cap where applicable.
Is the advertised APR temporary?Introductory period, intro APR and what happens when it ends.
What fees can apply?Annual, closing, appraisal, early-termination, fixed-rate conversion and state-specific charges.
How long can I draw?Draw period and minimum-payment rules during that phase.
What happens after the draw period?Repayment period and how principal and interest will be repaid.
How much could I borrow?Lender-specific CLTV/equity rules and the credit line actually offered to you.

Membership matters — but so does HELOC availability

Being able to join a credit union does not automatically mean its HELOC is available for your property. Membership eligibility and loan availability are separate questions. A nationally accessible membership path can coexist with state-specific lending restrictions.

Before opening an account solely because you are interested in a HELOC, confirm the current membership rules, whether HELOCs are offered where the property is located, and whether the property type you own is eligible.

Do credit unions have easier HELOC requirements?

There is no universal rule that credit unions approve HELOC applicants at a particular credit score, DTI or CLTV. Each institution establishes its own underwriting criteria within applicable law, and published product limits may have additional conditions.

That is why we have removed the old claims that “680+” is generally required, “720+” gets the best rates, or that borrowers should expect a universal DTI threshold. See our credit-score guide and qualification guide for the source-based approach we use across the site.

What should you take from this comparison?

A credit union can be a useful place to obtain a HELOC quote, but the institution's ownership structure does not tell you whether its offer will be cheaper or better suited to you. Compare the actual offer with alternatives using the same criteria: ongoing rate structure, fees, eligibility, borrowing limits and repayment terms.

Because lender products change, this page is deliberately designed as a research starting point rather than a permanent ranking. Follow the official lender links above for current terms before making an application decision.
