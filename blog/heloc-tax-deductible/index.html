Is HELOC Interest Tax-Deductible? What IRS Guidance Says

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026 13 min read Mike Lucas — Founder

In this guide

  1. The short answer
  2. Why the use of HELOC funds matters
  3. Check current IRS guidance
  4. When to speak with a tax professional
Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

HELOC interest may be deductible for U.S. federal income-tax purposes in some circumstances. The IRS focuses on factors including what the borrowed money was used for, whether the debt is secured by a qualified home, applicable debt limits and whether the taxpayer itemizes deductions.

This page is not a tax opinion and it does not try to decide whether your interest is deductible. Its job is to translate the current IRS material into plain English and point you to the official sources you can use to verify it.

The short answer

HELOC interest is not automatically tax-deductible. The IRS explains that interest may qualify in certain circumstances, depending on how the borrowed funds are used, the home securing the debt and other applicable requirements.

Start with the IRS

The IRS answer to whether HELOC interest is deductible is: "It depends." This guide is a general introduction, not a determination of anyone's tax eligibility.

Why the use of HELOC funds matters

Under the IRS guidance, interest may be deductible when qualifying borrowed funds are used to buy, build or substantially improve the home securing the loan, subject to applicable requirements and limits.

Using a HELOC for personal expenses, such as paying off credit-card debt, does not make that interest deductible as home-mortgage interest. Borrowers should not assume that every home improvement or borrowing arrangement qualifies.

Check current IRS guidance

Tax rules, limits and individual circumstances matter. Rather than relying on figures reproduced here, consult the official IRS resources for the relevant tax year:

  • IRS FAQ: Is HELOC interest deductible?
  • IRS Publication 936: Home Mortgage Interest Deduction
  • IRS Schedule A Instructions

When to speak with a tax professional

If you are considering claiming a deduction, speak with a qualified US tax professional who can review your circumstances and the rules applicable to your tax year.

Keep relevant loan documents and records showing how borrowed funds were used, and ask your tax professional what documentation may be needed.
