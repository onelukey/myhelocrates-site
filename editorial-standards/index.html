How We Work

Editorial Standards & Methodology

How we choose what to write about, how we verify what we publish, and what happens if we get something wrong.

MyHelocRates.com exists to help US homeowners understand Home Equity Lines of Credit — a financial product that is widely available but poorly explained. This page sets out, plainly, how the content on this site gets made: how we pick topics, where our numbers come from, how often we revisit them, and what to do if you spot an error.

Editorial independence: MyHelocRates.com has no affiliate relationships, lender partnerships, or paid placements. This site is funded by display advertising only. No lender or financial company has ever paid for or influenced any content, ranking, or recommendation on this site.

How we choose what to write about

Topics are chosen based on the real questions US homeowners actually have about HELOCs — drawn from search behaviour, common points of confusion identified in CFPB consumer complaint data, and gaps we notice in existing coverage elsewhere. We prioritise topics where clear, honest information is genuinely hard to find, rather than topics that are easy to monetise.

How we verify what we publish

Every factual claim involving a number, rate, or statistic is sourced from a primary or authoritative source — not from other blogs or secondary summaries. The table below shows the source categories we rely on most and what each one is used for.

Where our information comes from

Federal ReserveH.15 release (Prime Rate), FOMC calendar, G.19 Consumer Credit data
CFPBConsumer protection guidance, complaint data, regulatory context
IRSPublication 936 and official guidance for all tax-deductibility content
Lender rate surveysBankrate, Curinos, and LendingTree published national averages
Industry reportsZonda/Remodeling Magazine Cost vs. Value Report for renovation ROI data
Individual lendersPublished rate pages and terms, verified directly on the lender's own site

Where a figure cannot be sourced to one of the above during writing, it is flagged internally as needing a source rather than published unsupported. We do not use AI-generated statistics, estimates presented as facts, or numbers pulled from unverified aggregator sites.

How often content is reviewed and updated

HELOC rates move with Federal Reserve policy, and tax rules change with legislation. We review rate-related content whenever there is a material Fed rate decision, and review the rest of the site's content periodically — checking that IRS figures, standard deduction amounts, and legislative references (such as OBBBA provisions) remain current. Every page displays its last-updated date. We do not claim a fixed automated review schedule we don't actually run — if a page says "updated," it means a person reviewed it, not that a system did.

Corrections policy

Found something wrong?

If you spot a factual error, an outdated figure, or a broken source link anywhere on this site, please let us know using "Content correction" as the topic. We review every correction request personally. Confirmed errors are corrected as soon as practical, and for significant factual corrections we note the update date on the affected page.

Who writes this content

All content on MyHelocRates.com is researched and written by Mike Lucas, the site's founder — not by an editorial team or outsourced writers. You can read more about why the site exists and Mike's background on the About page.

Reminder: Nothing on this site constitutes financial, tax, or legal advice. Content is for general education. Always consult a qualified professional before making borrowing decisions. See our full Disclaimer.
