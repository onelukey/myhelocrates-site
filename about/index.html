A note from the founder

Why I Built MyHelocRates.com — and Why I'm Not American

My name is Mike Lucas, and I live in the UK. A few years ago I wanted to renovate my home. Like most people, I started looking into how to fund it — and I fell down a very interesting rabbit hole.

I came across the concept of a Home Equity Line of Credit. HELOCs do exist in the UK, but they are complicated, restricted, and rarely used. So I did what most UK homeowners do: I took out a traditional fixed personal loan. I borrowed a set amount upfront — whether I used every penny or not — and started paying interest on the full sum from day one. No flexibility. No ability to draw what I needed, when I needed it. Just a fixed repayment, every month, on money I hadn't yet spent.

Then I discovered how US HELOCs work: a revolving line of credit secured by the home, with borrowing available during a draw period under the terms of the agreement. The structure was very different from the fixed personal loan I had used, and it made me curious about how HELOCs actually work, what they cost, and where the risks sit.

What struck me next was how much there was to understand. A HELOC can be useful in some circumstances, but it is also debt secured by your home, with rates, fees, payment rules and lender requirements that can vary. I wanted one place that explained those moving parts clearly without treating a HELOC as automatically good or bad — and without starting from a sales pitch.

I'm not a financial adviser, mortgage broker or lender. I'm a researcher and editor with a long-standing interest in personal finance. I built my understanding from primary US sources — especially the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, Federal Reserve and IRS — and I use lender disclosures where a particular product or term needs to be described. The aim is to separate general HELOC concepts from lender-specific rules rather than turn one lender's criteria into a universal answer.

MyHelocRates.com is what I built from that research. It is an independent, plain-English educational resource about HELOCs. The site does not originate loans, broker applications or have lender relationships. The goal is to help US homeowners understand how HELOCs work, what to verify with a lender, and what risks to consider before making their own borrowing decisions.

If you're a US homeowner trying to understand a HELOC before deciding whether to explore one further, this site was built to give you a clearer starting point.

Mike Lucas

Editorial standards

What this site stands for

Three principles guide every page on MyHelocRates.com.

Full transparency

This site is supported by advertising. Advertising does not determine our editorial conclusions, and MyHelocRates.com does not originate loans or have lender relationships. If another form of commercial relationship is introduced in future, it will be clearly disclosed where relevant.

Education first

Every page is written to inform, not to sell. That means explaining why a HELOC might not be right for you just as clearly as why it might be — because a bad borrowing decision serves nobody.

Kept current

Pages are reviewed on a schedule appropriate to the subject. Time-sensitive information is dated when it is genuinely checked; evergreen pages are not given artificial monthly updates. Where a figure can change, we point readers to the relevant primary source or lender disclosures.

How we work

Where the information comes from

Our core HELOC guides are researched from primary US sources wherever possible, with lender materials used when discussing lender-specific products or terms.

  • Primary US sources

    For HELOC mechanics and consumer-credit rules, we use Consumer Financial Protection Bureau resources and federal regulations. For benchmark rate context, we use Federal Reserve data. Tax-related explanations are checked against current IRS guidance.

  • Lender terms stay lender-specific

    When a lender's rate, fee, eligibility rule or product feature is discussed, we treat it as that lender's information rather than an industry-wide rule. Readers should verify current offers and requirements directly with the lender before applying.

  • Plain-English explanation

    Primary sources can be technical. We turn the relevant material into plain-English explanations while linking back to authoritative sources so readers can verify important details for themselves.

  • Risks belong beside benefits

    A HELOC is secured by your home. If you cannot repay it, you could lose your home. We therefore explain borrowing risks, variable-rate uncertainty, fees and repayment obligations alongside potential uses and features.

  • Review dates mean something

    A “Last reviewed” date is changed only when the page and its supporting information have actually been checked. We do not automatically change article dates simply because the calendar month changes.
